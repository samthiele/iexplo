Outcrop mapping provides crucial geological information in the exploration, evaluation, mining and monitoring phases of mineral-resource development. It is also an essential part of quality and process control and geotechnical risk assessment. Traditionally, geologists map visually and in direct contact with the outcrop. That is time-consuming, inefficient and subjective, and it can be dangerous: recently blasted faces are often unstable.

Remote mapping of mine walls has therefore focused on two data types: (i) detailed three-dimensional outcrop models and (ii) two-dimensional hyperspectral images from which material composition can be derived. Our work merges high-resolution point clouds with hyperspectral images into *hyperclouds*. These datasets allow integrated representation, analysis and interpretation of geometric and spectral features.
