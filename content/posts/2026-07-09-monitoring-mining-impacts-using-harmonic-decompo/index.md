Global mining operations cause significant vegetation disturbance, but their cumulative impact over time is often difficult to quantify remotely. We developed Seasonal Harmonic Anomaly Break Analysis (SHABA), a new breakpoint detection approach, to integrate vegetation index time series and identify both abrupt and subtle vegetation changes associated with mining activities.

We demonstrated the potential of SHABA by applying it to six geographically distributed mine sites: Cardinal River, Roșia Poieni, Trident, Carajás, Vametco, and Worsley. The resulting breakpoints accurately quantify cumulative changes associated with mine expansion and rehabilitation efforts, and indicate where secondary changes in surrounding areas might occur.

We found varied and complex mine footprint trajectories, with primary footprints showing both stepwise vegetation clearing and early regrowth consistent with rehabilitation. Secondary changes outside the direct mine footprint varied widely but were temporally correlated with mine expansion. We suggest the SHABA workflow could provide a robust approach for automated, global-scale quantification of changes in mine footprint over time, improving environmental accounting and stakeholder dialogue.
