Volcanic rock masses are difficult to characterise due to their heterogeneous structures and the complex interplay of volcanic, hydrothermal, and geomorphological processes. We expanded on previous research by acquiring a unique dataset of visible-near (VNIR), shortwave (SWIR), midwave (MWIR), and longwave (LWIR) infrared reflectance from samples across eight basaltic to andesitic volcanoes.

We then trained several machine learning models to predict density, porosity, uniaxial compressive strength (UCS), and Young's modulus (E) from these spectral data. Non-linear models, particularly multilayer perceptrons (MLP), explained up to 80% of the variance in density and porosity, and 65–70% in UCS and E. Explainable AI analysis showed that VNIR-SWIR absorptions, attributable to hydrothermal alteration, and MWIR-LWIR features, sensitive to volcanic glass content and fabric, were the dominant contributors.

These results demonstrate that hyperspectral imaging can serve as a robust proxy for rock physical and mechanical properties, offering an efficient and scalable method for characterising large areas of exposed volcanic rock. Integrating these data with geomechanical models could significantly enhance hazard assessment, infrastructure development, and resource utilisation in volcanic regions.
