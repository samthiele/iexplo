Mineralogical data from drillcores are crucial for resource estimation and geometallurgical modelling, but hyperspectral imaging struggles with accurate quantification without extensive calibration. We built on previous work to combine scanning electron microscope (SEM) mineral maps with large-extent multimodal hyperspectral imaging data. Our goal was to relate hyperspectral features to mineral abundances using supervised machine learning models, then apply these models to infer mineralogy across entire drillcores.

We adapted the learning process for the unbalanced composition of most rocks, achieving reasonable accuracy for common rock-forming minerals. However, prediction accuracy strongly depended on training data representativity, often failing for rare and accessory minerals. We suggest robust, adaptive sampling approaches to address this, and emphasise that upscaled predictions must always be validated. Once validated, these predictions can provide crucial quantitative data, bridging the scale gap between petrographic observations, metallurgical tests, and geometallurgical models.
