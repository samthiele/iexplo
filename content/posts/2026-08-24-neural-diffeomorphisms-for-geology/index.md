Building accurate 3D structural geological models is crucial for understanding subsurface processes and resource exploration, but representing complex geological deformations remains a significant challenge. We introduce a new approach that leverages neural diffeomorphisms to model these intricate transformations.

Our method employs diffeomorphic registration, where the underlying smooth, invertible transformation is derived from a learned velocity field. This velocity field is constructed using Euler Potentials, a mathematical framework well-suited for describing geological structures, and parametrised by Random Fourier Feature series to capture intricate details.

This approach offers a powerful way to represent and model complex geological deformations, potentially leading to more accurate and robust 3D models of the subsurface. By integrating learned transformations, we aim to improve the efficiency and fidelity of structural geological modelling workflows.
